abstract: >-
  Korean hybrid mortgages reset automatically five years after origination on a predetermined date, and tight limits on cash-out borrowing make the reset a clean cash-flow shock. I exploit this quasi-experiment during the 2022–23 rate hikes, using a credit bureau panel. The MPC out of the higher interest payments is small, about 0.1, well below estimates from resets during rate cuts, because borrowers adjust mainly by refinancing or paying down their mortgages. However, liquidity-constrained borrowers are much less able to refinance or extend maturity, and they instead lose access to unsecured credit and cut card spending by about 3 percent relative to unconstrained borrowers. These results suggest that targeted access to refinancing and maturity extension can mitigate the distributional costs of monetary tightening.
summary: >-

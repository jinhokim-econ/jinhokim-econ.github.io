abstract: This paper studies how demographic composition shapes housing price cycles. Using cross-country and U.S. regional panel data, we show that areas with younger populations experience significantly larger housing cycles. We provide evidence that young households exhibit stronger delayed overreaction in housing price expectations, which amplifies price fluctuations. A Bayesian learning framework explains this mechanism -- less rigid priors among younger agents lead them to reinterpret shocks as persistent over time, generating cyclical amplification. 

# Summary. An optional shortened abstract.
summary: Why do some housing markets experience larger boom-bust cycles? We show that regions with younger populations exhibit stronger price fluctuations because young households overreact to housing market shocks. A Bayesian learning framework explains how their expectation dynamics amplify housing cycles.
